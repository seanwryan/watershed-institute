About StreamWatch & data use

How to interpret StreamWatch monitoring data in this application.

What this app shows

StreamWatch is a volunteer water-quality monitoring program. This web app provides:

  • Map & sites — Monitoring locations, waterbody names, and last sample dates.
  • Explore — Historical water chemistry and bacteria measurements (for example temperature, nitrate, dissolved oxygen, and E. coli), including optional comparisons and reference threshold lines.
  • Scores — Biological index scores (HGMI, NJIS, CPMI) from macroinvertebrate sampling.
  • QA — Summary of flagged results and a review table of flagged records by site, flag type, and date.
  • Export — Simple CSV downloads for analysis and WQX-style CSV for Water Quality Exchange workflows.
  • Operations — Volunteer profiles and CAT meter inventory/testing records for Watershed staff.

What this app does not do

Please keep the following in mind when using the data:

  • Review status — Results may carry a Data Condition tag. The Sep 9, 2026 Watershed Data Dictionary emphasizes tags such as Provisional, Flagged, Outlier, and Duplicate?. Older app lookup codes (for example Unchecked or Accepted) may still appear in stored records; see the QA page for Watershed vocabulary vs app organization.
  • Not for sole decision-making — Use this app for exploration, QA review, and reporting support. Do not rely on it alone for regulatory or management decisions without verifying data and QA status.
  • Coverage — Not all sites or years have every parameter or biological index; gaps reflect historical collection, not necessarily absence of concern.

Understanding data condition

Each result can have a data condition. Per the Sep 9, 2026 StreamWatch Data Dictionary, tags currently described for the raw dataset include:

  • Provisional — Preliminary; may still change.
  • Incomplete — Missing key elements or measurements.
  • Outlier — Outside the typical range; warrants a second look (not automatically wrong).
  • Minor Deviation — Small inconsistencies; generally usable as-is.
  • Flagged — Marked for review; Watershed practice excludes Flagged records from analysis.
  • Corrected — An identified issue was fixed.
  • Erroneous — Physically impossible value.
  • Duplicate / Duplicate? — Repeated entry (confirmed or still awaiting paper-sheet reconciliation).

This application may still store additional historical lookup codes (for example Accepted, Unchecked, Validated). Those are app/database values, not the Sep 9 Dictionary’s current raw-workbook vocabulary. See the QA page for both views.

Key parameters

Brief explanations of parameters you will see in Explore and on site pages:

  • Water temperature (°C) — In-stream temperature. High values (for example above 31 °C) can stress aquatic life and may be flagged as exceedances.
  • Dissolved oxygen (mg/L) — Oxygen available to aquatic life. Low dissolved oxygen often correlates with high temperature or nutrient loading.
  • Nitrate — Often reported in µg/L. Elevated nitrate can indicate nutrient pollution.
  • Phosphate — A phosphorus form that can drive algal growth; reported in mg/L.
  • Chloride (mg/L) — Can increase from road salt and other sources; relevant for freshwater standards.
  • E. coli (MPN/100 mL) — Fecal indicator bacteria; high counts may indicate impaired contact recreation.
  • pH — Acidity/alkalinity; typically stable in many streams but useful for quality checks.
  • Turbidity (NTU) — Cloudiness; can spike after storms and affect habitat and clarity.

More information

For questions about interpreting StreamWatch results, QA flags, or exports, contact Watershed Institute staff. Thresholds and review practices follow the program’s established monitoring guidance.
